How the Campus Expansion Priority Score is calculated

Each of the five indicators is normalized to 0–100 and clamped to its fixed reference range. Higher-is-better metrics use 100 × (value − minimum) ÷ (maximum − minimum); lower-is-better metrics use 100 × (maximum − value) ÷ (maximum − minimum). The priority score is the weighted sum of the four business-condition subtotals.

Campus score = Market Size + Market Need + Market Attractiveness + Operational Feasibility

Four business conditions, five indicators

Strategic assumptions

Condition definitions, metric directions, and weights are strategic assumptions, not statistically validated predictors of SNAG's success. Walk Score measures pedestrian connectivity, not scooter permissions or actual delivery times.

Weights and missing data

Weights shown are the defaults. A new set applies only when its integer percentages total exactly 100%. Missing or invalid values receive no score or contribution, and remaining weights are not redistributed; totals are provisional when data is missing. Out-of-range values are clamped, and ranges do not adapt to the selected schools.

Data scope and limitations

Prospective scores use only the 19 candidates' metric values; the 12 existing SNAG campuses do not affect them. Median family income's $72,300–$187,300 range was frozen using all 31 researched campuses. Pell Grant percentage, grocery-store count, and grocery distance are not scored. The score does not predict demand, revenue, profitability, or success and should not alone determine an expansion decision.
